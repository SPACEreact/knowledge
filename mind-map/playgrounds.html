Visual Studies

Read the relationships slowly. Each plate isolates one visual principle so the eye can compare before language explains.

Cinematography

01

Three-Point Lighting

The key establishes direction, the fill controls contrast, and the back light separates the subject. Their ratio shapes shadow, highlight and emotional pressure.

Cinematography

02

Focal Length Psychology

Compare 18mm wide and expansive, 50mm natural, and 200mm compressed. Focal length changes field of view; changing camera distance changes perspective and the background relationship.

Cinematography

03

Depth of Field

Compare f/1.4 with its shallow focus and soft bokeh to f/16 with deeper focus. Aperture works together with focal length, sensor size and subject distance to control what remains sharp.

Cinematography

04

Colour Temperature

Move conceptually from warm candlelight (2000K) to cool blue sky (10000K). Colour temperature can suggest intimacy, neutrality, distance or unease before the story names the feeling.

Design

05

Composition Guides

Compare how the same visual weight behaves inside rule-of-thirds, golden-ratio and diagonal structures. A guide is useful when it clarifies intention, not when it becomes a formula.

Motion

06

Easing Curves

Compare linear motion with ease-in-out and overshoot. The same start and end points can feel mechanical, graceful or energetic depending on how velocity changes between them.

Editing

07

Editing Rhythm

Compare long, even and accelerating shot lengths. Cut placement creates pulse; alignment with a beat can reinforce momentum, while cutting against it can create tension or surprise.

Sound

08

Sound Layers

Four layers—dialogue, music, effects and ambience—shape a scene's emotional texture. Clarity comes from hierarchy: every layer should not compete at equal intensity.
